Competitive intelligence

Neuroscience assets, read against the primary record.

Competitive analysis in this field mostly restates what sponsors say about themselves. This practice reads the label, the registry entry, the patent and the paper, then says which of those a claim rests on.

Who it is for

Founders and business development leads deciding whether a competitor's disclosure changes their own plan. Investors who need a neuro asset's evidence tiered before they price it.

What makes the analysis different

Primary records, or nothing

Trade coverage and databases find programmes. They never support a claim. One Nature Reviews feature led to three programmes later verified in the registry, and was the sole source for a set of platform holders that closed against no company document and were struck from every count. Both outcomes are logged.

Nature Reviews Drug Discovery news feature, 16 July 2026, followed through to ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05756920, NCT05704361 and NCT06475404.

Every mechanism claim carries a confidence tier

HIGH is a primary document. MEDIUM is an inference, written as one. LOW is secondary coverage only, and LOW rows stay in the file marked rather than deleted. AbbVie name no receptor for ALIA-1758, so it sits at LOW with its Phase 1 complete.

AbbVie press release, 11 December 2024; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06406348.

Human data is never set beside animal data

The 55-fold target engagement figure behind Roche's Brainshuttle module is a mouse result. Denali's 10 to 30-fold brain exposure figure is from animal models. Neither shares a table with a clinical readout.

Niewoehner et al., Neuron 2014; Denali Therapeutics press release, 25 March 2026.
