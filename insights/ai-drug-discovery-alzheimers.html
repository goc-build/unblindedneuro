Progress of artificial intelligence-driven drug discovery in Alzheimer's disease

1 November 2026. Data cut 29 October 2026.

I can trace 5 assets from computational drug discovery into clinical development for indications relevant to Alzheimer's disease. The 2 that target the disease itself came from physics-based simulation and transcriptomic signature matching, while the 3 that came from machine learning treat behavioural symptoms or have not yet been tested in Alzheimer's disease.

Five computationally derived assets are in the clinic for indications relevant to Alzheimer's disease

Of the 5 clinical-stage assets credited to computational discovery, the 2 that target Alzheimer's disease pathology came from methods other than machine learning; the 3 from machine learning treat psychosis or agitation, or are being developed first in Parkinson's disease.

Across tau, amyloid-beta, apolipoprotein E, neuroinflammation and neuropsychiatric programmes, I found 5 assets whose sponsors or originating researchers credit computation with finding either the molecule or its Alzheimer's indication, and which have a registered clinical trial or a company-reported clinical stage. For scale, the 2026 Alzheimer's disease drug development pipeline counts 158 drugs in 192 active trials on ClinicalTrials.gov at 1 January 2026, of which 116 (73%) are disease-targeted therapies and 16 (10%) are symptom-targeted therapies for neuropsychiatric symptoms. Of the 5 assets below, only bumetanide and PMN310 appear in that count.

Machine learning has reached the clinic in Alzheimer's disease through symptoms and through a target not yet tested in Alzheimer's patients

The 3 machine-learning-derived assets solved chemistry or indication-selection problems on established targets: 2 treat behavioural symptoms of Alzheimer's dementia, and the third, an NLRP3 inhibitor, is being developed first in Parkinson's disease.

Sumitomo describes DSP-0038 as a single molecule that antagonises the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor and agonises the 5-HT1A receptor while avoiding the dopamine D2 receptor, the selectivity problem the machine learning design work addressed. It has been listed at Phase 1 since May 2021, the 2026 pipeline report lists no active trial for it, and I found no disclosed human data.

BioXcel's Form 10-Q describes its approach as applying machine learning to approved or clinically evaluated drugs to identify new indications, and dexmedetomidine was already approved as an intravenous sedative. The same filing records that BioXcel let its option on its former parent's EvolverAI engine lapse, with no development work done by the parent after December 2024. BioXcel announced the TRANQUILITY In-Care Phase 3 (60 µg, 12 weeks, care-home setting) in November 2025, reported substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern in mid-2026, and sold BXCL501 and IGALMI to Teva through a Section 363 bankruptcy sale for $57.5m upfront plus up to $67.5m in contingent payments. Teva's release names only at-home use in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, with no mention of Alzheimer's disease. A separate dexmedetomidine programme from Teikoku Pharma USA is in Phase 2 for agitation in Alzheimer's disease (NCT06052254), with no attribution to artificial intelligence.

ISM8969 is the only machine-learning-designed molecule I found against a disease mechanism relevant to Alzheimer's disease. Insilico generated and optimised it with Chemistry42 as a brain-penetrant NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor and dosed the first participant in June 2026, in a Phase 1 that plans to enrol 80 healthy participants and 20 obese adults at risk of cardiovascular disease, with cerebrospinal fluid sampling to measure brain penetration. Insilico's lead indication is Parkinson's disease; Alzheimer's disease appears in its materials as a disease in which NLRP3 activation contributes to pathology, not as a planned indication.

The 2 disease-targeted assets came from physics-based simulation and transcriptomic signature matching

PMN310 and bumetanide are the only disease-targeted Alzheimer's assets in the clinic credited to computational discovery, and neither method is machine learning, so whether they count as artificial intelligence-driven is up for debate.

ProMIS Neurosciences' PMN310 is a humanised immunoglobulin G1 antibody against amyloid-beta oligomers. ProMIS's annual filing states that development of PMN310 began with its computational platform, whose EpiSelect algorithm "combines physics and biology to simulate the folding, or misfolding of proteins". PRECISE-AD (NCT06750432) is a 144-patient Phase 1b in mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer's disease or mild Alzheimer's disease, and the blinded 6-month interim in July 2026 reported no amyloid-related imaging abnormalities with oedema in 136 patients. Because those data are pooled across arms, no efficacy can be read from them; unblinded 12-month topline, including cognitive outcomes, is guided for the first quarter of 2027.

Bumetanide came from a 2021 screen by Taubes and colleagues at the Gladstone Institutes and the University of California, San Francisco, which matched apolipoprotein E4-dependent Alzheimer's transcriptomic signatures from human brain tissue against the drug-induced transcriptomic profiles in the Connectivity Map, then found lower Alzheimer's prevalence among bumetanide users over 65 in electronic health records. Stanford University is now running a 40-participant Phase 2a in mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to Alzheimer's disease (NCT06052163), with safety and tolerability as the primary outcome and primary completion estimated for December 2026.

A third computationally nominated drug, sildenafil, came from Cleveland Clinic's 2021 network medicine analysis of 1,608 approved drugs, which also reported a 69% lower Alzheimer's risk among sildenafil users in insurance claims data covering 7.23 million people. The authors planned a Phase 2 trial, but sildenafil has no active trial in the 2026 pipeline report.

Machine learning has shortened chemistry, but Alzheimer's programmes have mostly failed at the trial bottleneck

Machine learning has so far shortened the chemistry stage, whereas disease-targeted Alzheimer's programmes have mostly failed in trials, on target choice and on enrolling patients whose biology does not match the mechanism.

Cummings et al. list better target identification and better selection of potentially responsive patients among the changes expected to improve the success rate of Alzheimer's drug development, and neither is a chemistry problem. The one direct test I found is consistent with this: a machine learning prognostic model applied retrospectively to AMARANTH, the lanabecestat Phase 2/3 stopped for futility, reported a significant treatment effect on primary outcomes in a model-defined subgroup. The reanalysis is post-hoc and covers a single trial; the data licensed in the Bristol Myers Squibb and Pathos AI deal would allow the same approach to be tested prospectively.

Cummings et al. 2026, Discussion; AI-guided patient stratification improves outcomes and efficiency in the AMARANTH Alzheimer's Disease clinical trial, PMC12271323, analysing NCT02245737.

The trial bottleneck: my view across indications

This section is my opinion, built on the data above.

The data above shows that large pharmaceutical companies are not treating machine-learning-driven drug design as a solution on its own. They have invested in drug design, but I expect their next wave of spending to go into trial design optimisation, as Bristol Myers Squibb's deal with Pathos AI already shows, because however much artificial intelligence shortens drug design, the trial process that follows remains the longest stage.

This applies most to Alzheimer's disease, where trials are long and recruitment-heavy, but it holds across indications: the problem to solve is the balance between trial efficiency and patient safety. Regulators in Alzheimer's have already moved. The United States Food and Drug Administration granted lecanemab accelerated approval in January 2023 on the basis of amyloid reduction, converted it to traditional approval in July 2023 after the confirmatory trial, and its 2024 draft guidance on early Alzheimer's disease covers the use of surrogate endpoints. Accelerated approvals carry risk, as aducanumab showed when Biogen discontinued it in January 2024 and terminated its confirmatory study, so confidence in them should grow with each surrogate a confirmatory trial validates, not with the number granted.

Methods that reduce the human data a trial needs offer a second route. Digital twin-style prognostic models already let sponsors shrink control arms with regulator approval, and the faster methods like these reduce how much in-human data a trial needs, the faster new therapeutics reach patients.

Method and limits

This is a minimum count from public records, not a complete census.

  • Inclusion: the sponsor or originating researchers state that the molecule, or its Alzheimer's indication, was identified computationally, and a company filing or registry record places it in clinical development for an indication relevant to Alzheimer's disease.
  • I searched tau, amyloid-beta, apolipoprotein E, neuroinflammation, phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitor and neuropsychiatric programmes across company filings, press releases, ClinicalTrials.gov and peer-reviewed literature, up to 29 October 2026.
  • Excluded: AriBio's AR1001 (mirodenafil), a repurposed phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitor in Phase 3 (NCT05531526) and due to read out in 2026. Startup databases such as Dealroom tag AriBio as an artificial intelligence drug discovery company, but AriBio does not credit computation with the Alzheimer's indication. Also excluded are computational nominations with no trial.
  • I have not counted artificial intelligence-first Alzheimer's companies founded, because no primary register exists and aggregator counts depend on how firms label themselves; AR1001 shows how far that labelling can sit from the origin of the drug.

Cummings et al. 2026, Table 1 and Section 3.12; Dealroom company profile, AriBio, accessed 29 October 2026, used as the example of aggregator labelling only.
